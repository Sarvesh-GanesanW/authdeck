One tap for the things you do all day

You start with four pages, Code, Git, Run and Apps, set up for VS Code on your system. Tap Edit to move keys around, change what they do or add your own. Long-press a key for its menu.

  • Open an app. The picker lists what's installed on your computer, icons included.
  • Press a shortcut, after bringing a chosen app to the front if you like.
  • Run a command in a new terminal window, or quietly in the background with its output and exit code on the key.
  • Type a snippet of text.
  • Open a link, a file or a folder.
  • Do several of these in a row.
  • Switch pages on its own when you change apps on your computer.

Three colour schemes come with it: Graphite, Paper and Tide. The squares at the top of this page switch between them.

On your computer

    Two-factor codes that type themselves

    Your codes are made on your phone or tablet, with no network needed. Put one on the deck and tap it: the computer types the digits into whatever field you're in.

    • Works with most sites (TOTP), counter codes (HOTP) and Steam Guard.
    • Add an account by scanning its QR code, typing its key, or importing from Google Authenticator's Transfer accounts code.
    • The computer gets only the digits, only when you tap. It keeps an encrypted backup of your vault that it can't read.
    • A 12-word recovery phrase brings your codes back on a new phone.
    • Lock the app with Face ID, your fingerprint or your passcode.

    Your .env secrets, in the repo and released from your phone

    An env set keeps a project's secrets in its own git repo, in one file. Names are readable, values are encrypted so only your team's phones and tablets can open them, and every line is signed by the device that wrote it.

    • Values go into one process. authdeck env run asks your phone, and nothing is written to disk. Allow for 15 minutes lets the next runs on that computer through without a tap.
    • A lost laptop gives up only ciphertext. The computer never keeps a set's key, so all it holds is the encrypted file and any .env you pulled onto it.
    • Teammates join from the repo. They ask with a request file, and before you approve, you each read out six key words and pick the other's from three choices.
    • Removing someone makes a new key and lists the variables to rotate. The repo carries the file; there's no server.

    Set up in three steps

    1. 1

      Install the agent

      It sits in the menu bar or tray and does what your keys ask. macOS, Windows and Linux.

    2. 2

      Install the app

      Android today. iPhone and iPad aren't on the App Store yet.

    3. 3

      Pair them

      The agent shows a QR code and a six-digit PIN. Scan the code, or pick your computer and type the PIN. Both need to be on the same Wi-Fi.

    Your devices, your network, no account

    The app talks straight to the agent over your own Wi-Fi. The link is encrypted, and both ends prove who they are with keys exchanged when you pair. Each PIN attempt is one guess, and three wrong PINs bring a new PIN and a pause.

    A paired device can run commands as you, so pair only devices you own, and unpair a lost one from the agent's window.

    What it doesn't protect against

    Other programs running as you on the same computer can change the agent's settings and paired devices. Keep that computer free of malware.

    The builds aren't signed with paid certificates yet, so your system asks for an extra click the first time you open them. The steps are under the downloads.

    The first time you open it

    macOS
    Open the app once, then go to System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. Turn on AuthDeck Agent under Accessibility when macOS asks: shortcuts and typing need it.
    Windows
    When SmartScreen stops it, click More info, then Run anyway. When the firewall asks, allow it on private networks.
    Linux
    Shortcuts and typing need xdotool on X11, or wtype or ydotool on Wayland.
    Android
    Open the APK on the device. Android asks you to allow installs from your browser first.
    iPhone and iPad
    Not on the App Store yet. The unsigned IPA is for sideloading tools that sign it with your own Apple ID.
